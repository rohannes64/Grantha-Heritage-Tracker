Finding related books across scripts

The problem: Standard multilingual embeddings cluster by script first: a Sanskrit commentary and its Bengali or Tamil counterpart sit in separate islands, with only 2.7% of raw 10-nearest neighbours crossing scripts.

How Grantha solves it: Subtracting each language’s mean vector removes script bias while preserving topic structure. Script NMI drops from 0.642 to 0.065 on training books, lifting cross-script neighbours from 2.7% to 37.2%.

Identifying loose pages without metadata

The problem: Digitised archives contain loose pages and damaged title leaves with missing catalogue records, rotated scans, or degraded ink that causes silent misfiling.

How Grantha solves it: An incoming image is auto-rotated, read with Tesseract 5 across seven scripts, gated at 20 clean tokens so unreadable scans abstain (2.6% on test), and matched to its book’s group 75.2% of the time (vs 35.9% chance).

Rapid shelf-code suggestion & duplicate detection

The problem: Thousands of scanned volumes lack subject shelf codes or appear as unlinked duplicate editions under slightly different transliterated titles.

How Grantha solves it: Distance-weighted voting over the 25 nearest coded books puts the true catalogue class in the top three suggestions 91.6% of the time, while tight embedding proximity flags duplicate editions at 100.0% precision.

Top-3 class suggestion
91.6% (1,168 test books)
Edition finder precision
100.0% (36.4% recall)
