## Slide 1: Grantha Heritage Tracker

[RAJITH SUNDAR - SLIDE 1]

Grantha Heritage Tracker, Smart India Hackathon problem SIH26096, section K3P24XC.

A grantha is a palm-leaf manuscript, like the model on this slide: we drew it for the deck, and its leaves carry the opening couplets of the Tirukkural.

Our books are their printed descendants: 15,288 books harvested across 8 languages, 13,358 usable after the OCR quality gate. We embed them with language-centred Vertex AI Multilingual embeddings (100-d PCA) and cluster them with Ward (k = 3) and DBSCAN (2 dense cores, 30.7% noise).

The team: Rajith Sundar built the data and OCR pipeline, Etikala Sai Pranav ran the clustering study, and Vemuri Rohan built the app and delivered it.

## Slide 2: 15,288 books harvested, 13,358 clean enough to cluster

[RAJITH SUNDAR - SLIDE 2]

We harvested 15,288 books (77,634 OCR pages) across 8 languages from the Internet Archive's Digital Library of India scans. 13,358 (87.4%) passed the quality gate: a mean OCR word confidence of 50 or more and at least 100 tokens. 1,930 were set aside.

The books are split 8,129 train, 2,638 validation and 2,591 test, and the test split is locked.

Panel D is the leakage audit: scanning centre, source library, subject field, classification and publisher are dropped from the features, so the groups follow the text, not where a book was scanned.

If asked: Telugu (median confidence 55.4%) and Sanskrit (61.0%) lose the most books at the gate.

## Slide 3: From scanned pixels to script-free 100-d vectors

[RAJITH SUNDAR - SLIDE 3]

Five stages. One, read the OCR: the archive's hOCR for 10,581 books, its DjVu text for 1,682, and our own Tesseract pages for 1,095.

Two, clean: Unicode NFC normalisation, speckle removal and a per-language stopword cap. Three, the usability gate. Four, embed with Vertex AI Multilingual embeddings: 768-d vectors, compressed to 100-d with PCA, keeping 78.6% of the variance.

Five, centre by language: subtract each language's mean. On the 8,129 train books script NMI drops from 0.642 to 0.065, and a book's 10 nearest neighbours in the same script drop from 97% to 63%.

Walk the DFD: the text corpus goes through cleaning to the usability filter; the metadata is reviewed, imputed and encoded; usable books are embedded, centred and reduced, and land in the feature store with the encoded metadata block.

## Slide 4: Ward linkage on 100-d features selects k = 3

[ETIKALA SAI PRANAV - SLIDE 4]

We searched the hierarchical settings: Ward, average and complete linkage, each from k = 2 to 15. Euclidean distance won 3 to 1 over Manhattan.

Ward linkage at k = 3 on the 100-d features gives a silhouette of 0.035: a diffuse thematic manifold, not tight balls. Script NMI is 0.019, so the groups are essentially not language groups. The largest group holds 46.1% of the books and the smallest 1,714.

Panel A is the last merges of the dendrogram, with the dashed line at the k = 3 cut. Panel B is the silhouette across linkages and k.

If asked about the low silhouette: book space is continuous, so the groups are broad shelves, not islands.

## Slide 5: DBSCAN finds 2 dense cores and sets 30.7% aside as noise

[ETIKALA SAI PRANAV - SLIDE 5]

We swept 24 DBSCAN settings: MinPts 5, 10, 15, 20, and eps from 0.681 to 1.061.

The selected setting, MinPts 15 and eps 0.866, finds 2 dense cores and marks 30.7% of the books as noise. On re-runs it is very steady: bootstrap ARI 0.855.

The k-distance curves have no sharp knee, so eps is chosen from the sweep, where the noise share and the cluster count both settle.

If asked: noise is the label -1, the books outside every dense core. They are set aside, not forced into a group.

## Slide 6: 2D and 3D PCA across all 13,358 books: centring dissolves script walls

[ETIKALA SAI PRANAV - SLIDE 6]

The PCA projections cover all 13,358 usable books; the 100-d features keep 78.6% of the variance.

Panel A, before centring: the books cluster by script. Panel B, after language centring: all 8 languages mix across the 3 Ward groups. Panel C is the 3D centred projection.

## Slide 7: Language centring lifts subject agreement across every encoder

[ETIKALA SAI PRANAV - SLIDE 7]

The key finding: subtracting each language's centroid removes script clustering without losing the topic signal.

On the 8,129 train books, script NMI falls from 0.642 to 0.065, and a book's 10 nearest neighbours in the same script fall from 97% to 63%.

The right panel tests centring on four encoders: Gemini, BGE-M3, LaBSE and IndicSBERT. Seven-nearest-neighbour subject agreement on the labelled train and validation books rises for every one of them once they are centred.

The production model is Vertex AI Multilingual embeddings with 100-d PCA, across all 13,358 usable books.

## Slide 8: 3 broad cross-script groups, each mixing all 8 languages

[VEMURI ROHAN - SLIDE 8]

These are the Ward k = 3 groups on all 13,358 books. Each row is a group; the cells show each language's share of it, darker for a bigger share. No language holds more than 22% of any group.

Group 1: 6,152 books, mostly literature. Group 2: 1,714 books, mostly religion and philosophy. Group 3: 5,492 books, mostly religion and philosophy.

The groups act as broad archival shelves, not fine-grained Dewey classes.

## Slide 9: Validated on 2,591 held-out books: +14.5 points over a script-only baseline

[VEMURI ROHAN - SLIDE 9]

The test set of 2,591 books was locked and used once.

Page-to-book agreement: 75.2% of single pages land in their own book's group, against 35.9% by chance. Our target was 80%, so this one is not met.

Subject accuracy on 117 labelled test books: 53.0%, against 38.5% for guessing from the script: +14.5 points, p = 0.0005 over 2,000 label permutations.

Script NMI on the full corpus is 0.019, against 0.642 before centring.

Bootstrap ARI over 20 re-runs on 80% of the books: DBSCAN cores 0.855; the Ward groups 0.357 on train books and 0.260 on all books.

If asked about the Ward ARI: the three Ward groups are broad shelves, so books near their borders move when the data is resampled. The DBSCAN cores are the stable part, which is why we use both.

## Slide 10: Known limits, guardrails and responsible deployment

[VEMURI ROHAN - SLIDE 10]

Known limits. OCR quality: Telugu (median 55.4%) and Sanskrit (61.0%) lose the most books at the 50% gate. Group boundaries: the Ward bootstrap ARI is 0.357 on train books and 0.260 on all books, so the edges shift on resampling, while the DBSCAN cores hold at 0.855. Coarse catalogue codes: only 6,825 of the 13,358 books carry one, and on 1,168 coded test books the groups score 47.8% against 51.8% for guessing from the script. Genre overlap: two of the three groups lean to religion and philosophy, and hard cuts simplify blended treatises.

Guardrails. The app abstains on thin or noisy pages instead of guessing silently: 2.6% of test pages. The groups assist archivists and never reclassify library records on their own. Every suggestion shows its nearest cross-script neighbours and distances for human review. And scripts/check_claims.py traces every number in the deck to a result file.

Grantha is a working, reproducible system: it harvests, centres, clusters and explains 13,358 heritage books across 8 languages.
